The mechanic

One key. Nothing to learn.

You never say “new line” or “comma.” You hold a key, you talk, you let go. Everything between those two moments happens on your machine.

  1. 01

    Hold

    Right Option by default, or any key you like. A small pill appears by your cursor and never steals focus from what you’re typing into. It stays when you close the Velora window; hide it from its right-click menu or the menubar.

    ⌥
  2. 02

    Talk

    Ramble. Pause. Change your mind halfway through a sentence. Velora is built for the way people actually speak, not the way dictation software wishes they did.

  3. 03

    Release

    Cleanup runs locally and the text lands at your cursor. It stays on the clipboard too, so nothing is lost if an app refuses the paste.

    lands right here

Destination-aware

The app you’re in decides the format.

Velora reads which app is in front (and in Safari, Chrome or Firefox, which site) and switches its rules. Slack gets a message. Gmail gets paragraphs. Notion gets a note. Your terminal gets the command back verbatim, with no trailing period to break it.

Message

Slack · Messages · WhatsApp

“are we still on for thursday i can move things around if not”

Are we still on for Thursday? I can move things around if not

Terse. No invented greeting. A short line keeps its missing full stop.

Email

Mail · Gmail · Outlook

“thanks for sending that over i read it last night separately we should talk about the pricing page before friday”

Thanks for sending that over — I read it last night.

Separately, we should talk about the pricing page before Friday.

Paragraph breaks where the topic shifts. Never invents a sign-off you didn’t say.

Note

Notes · Notion · Obsidian

“first pull the logs second check the retry counter third file the ticket”

1. Pull the logs
2. Check the retry counter
3. File the ticket

Spoken ordinals become a numbered list. Plain prose stays prose.

Code

VS Code · Cursor · Terminal

“git commit dash m fix the retry backoff”

git commit -m "fix the retry backoff"

Verbatim. Nothing capitalised, no trailing period — a period breaks a shell command.

Stream Typing

Watch it type. Fix it while you talk.

Press ⌃⇧S and Velora shows a live draft as you speak, follows corrections like “3 p.m., no, 6 p.m.,” and finishes with clean text.

Editors update at your cursor. Terminals show the draft in Velora, then insert it when you finish.

Action Mode

Say what you want done. Keep working.

Hold ⌃⇧A and say “message Priya on Slack that I’m running late.” Velora plans the steps on your Mac and carries them out in the app you’re in: a few at a time, reading what the window actually says, stopping rather than guessing. Install the open-source Cua Driver and it can drive another app in a background window while you stay in yours. Say “draft” and it leaves the message written but unsent.

Meeting notes

Records the room. Never the screen.

After you confirm — every time — Velora captures microphone and computer audio, keeps a visible recording indicator running, and writes a transcript and notes you can search locally. It captures audio only.

Install

Two minutes to your first sentence.

Download the signed, notarised app or install it with Homebrew. On first launch Velora asks for microphone, input-monitoring and accessibility permissions, then downloads the models it needs — once. After that, dictation works with the Wi-Fi off.

Straight answers

Before you install.

Does Velora ever touch the network?

Models download once during setup, and optional update checks contact GitHub. During dictation, speech recognition and cleanup run locally — your audio and transcript are not uploaded. Update checks can be turned off.

Does it work offline?

Yes. Once the models are downloaded, dictation, Stream Typing, Voice Edit, Proofread and meeting notes all work with no connection at all.

What Mac do I need?

An Apple Silicon Mac running macOS 14 or later. Disk use depends on which speech and cleanup models you pick; Velora chooses a cleanup tier based on how much RAM you have.

Is it really free?

Yes. It is MIT-licensed and open source, with no account, no subscription, and no paid tier. There is nothing to upsell you because there is no server to pay for.

Which apps does it work in?

Any Mac app with a text field: Velora pastes at your cursor, so Mail, Slack, Notes, VS Code, browsers and terminals all work. If an app refuses the paste, the text is on your clipboard.

Is the iPhone app in the App Store?

No — and it is worth being blunt: the iPhone app is alpha and unstable. The Xcode project is in the repository and builds on iOS 17 or later, but it is unfinished, changes without warning, and is not something to rely on. The Mac app is the real product.

How do I get help?

Email sushilk.1991@gmail.com or open a GitHub issue. Velora is pre-1.0 and actively developed; bug reports genuinely help.
