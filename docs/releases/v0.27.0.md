# Velora 0.27.0

Velora now transcribes dictations of up to an hour and meetings of any
length, and the pill shows how far along a long dictation is.

## Dictation

- A dictation of up to an hour is transcribed and cleaned in parts,
  then inserted as one text.
- While a long dictation finishes, the pill shows its progress, such
  as "Transcribing 3 of 12" and "Cleaning 2 of 5". The shortcut starts
  a new dictation only once it's done, and Esc cancels it.
- If part of a dictation can't be transcribed, Velora inserts the rest
  and says how much is missing, for example "Part of this dictation
  couldn't be transcribed (90 seconds)". The History entry says so
  too.
- If transcription freezes, Velora restarts its speech engine. With
  "Keep audio recordings" on, the dictation comes back in History.
- With automatic language detection, Velora recognizes Indian-accented
  English more reliably and no longer reads English as Icelandic. On
  2,016 test clips, 15 of the 17 English clips it used to read as
  another language now come out in English.
- Pauses and breaths at the end of a dictation are skipped before
  transcription instead of after, so they no longer take extra time.
- Subtitle credits that Whisper sometimes invents during silence, such
  as "Closed Captioning by …", are no longer learned as dictionary
  words.

## Meetings

- Meetings of any length are transcribed. Velora reads a recording a
  piece at a time. In a test, a 10-hour recording peaked at about
  80 MB of memory. Recordings over four hours used to be refused.
- If a recording can't be read for a moment, Velora tries again later.
  One that truly can't be read is skipped, and the other side of the
  call still gets notes.

## Reliability

- Quitting Velora or switching the writing model no longer leaves a
  cleanup process running in the background.
- Everything still runs on this Mac.

## Upgrade

Replace the existing app in Applications with the new DMG, or update
in-app. Preferences, history, modes, and dictionary entries are
preserved.
