# English-first LFM3 scale experiment — 7 November 2026

Keep the production models unchanged. The selected 1.7B native candidate cuts English-only paired cleanup p50 from **558 ms to 264 ms** and samples a much smaller cleanup-process footprint, but it introduces new material-error cases on the owner's recordings. It remains experimental. The source change to ship from this round is the generic email prompt and its migration; the datasets and evaluation tooling are also retained.

The larger corpus has **7,631 reviewed English rows**. The original fixed-prompt challenge favored the new adapter strongly, but a post-hoc prompt control explains much of the old adapter's named-identity errors. Removing the examples reduced old C's email material flags from **40/51 to 2/51**, matching new G's 2/51; C still added 40 generic closings, while G added none. More data improved instruction compliance, but the headline fact-error comparison must not be presented as a broad semantic-reasoning gain.

Phonon remains faster on the matched short-cleanup workload (**33 ms versus 63 ms**). The new cleanup adapter failed meeting-summary qualification. Use the existing C++/Metal path for further English cleanup work; resolve the owner regressions before changing defaults, and treat summarization as a separate task.


On the pinned Phonon path, thinking is explicitly disabled. Its speed is evidence about a persistent, specialized cleanup engine with copy/draft work, not fast long-form reasoning. See the [source audit](2026-11-05-phonon-efficiency-audit.md) for the verified mechanisms and comparison limits.

This round asks whether a larger, independently reviewed English cleanup corpus improves the 1.7B Liquid model without adding words or losing needed repairs. Evaluation separates synthetic written English variants, publisher-labelled accent recordings, and the owner's frozen real dictations. Production model defaults are unchanged. Measurements use this Mac: Apple M5 Max, 36 GiB unified memory, macOS 27.0.1. No cross-chip or minimum-RAM qualification is implied.

## What changed in the data

The previous training corpus contained 2,216 total examples, including 1,466 English examples (1,233 train / 233 dev). The new larger English corpus contains 7,631 examples: 6,204 train / 1,427 dev. It preserves the smaller 3,101-example experiment byte-for-byte and preserves the original English examples and split assignments. It contains 4,492 effective connected components, not 7,631 independent scenarios. There are 3,332 exact identity examples and 79 examples with the production previous-text context suffix. The longest connected family has 15 rows.

Muse through YoYo generated 3,072 new labels for raw-only inputs from the pinned MIT-licensed Sotto corpus. Independent Terra reviews approved 2,839, rejected 233, and edited 403 targets. No upstream target column or Qwen cleanup output was used as a new teacher label. The upstream [Sotto source card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/juanquivilla/sotto-transcript-cleanup/blob/183cc8fd58532f13fa192980185214de1bcd5acc/README.md) describes a synthetic corpus built from programmatic corruption, model generation and handcrafted examples; these raw inputs are not certified natural human dictations. Its original generation included larger Qwen and Grok models, while this round uses only its input column and new Muse/Terra labels. Muse also authored 512 synthetic examples: 500 survived independent review. Those include regional spelling and lexical choices, literal identifiers, self-corrections, negative constraints, context continuation, and longer dictations. Synthetic regional labels indicate intended coverage; they do not certify authentic dialect or accent performance.

Identity augmentation takes an approved cleaned target and makes the same text both input and target under the same prompt contract, with linked provenance and family isolation. These derived examples teach preservation; they are not independently authored or independently labelled scenarios. Shared facts, paraphrases, contrast pairs, and eight-word overlaps stay in the same connected component. New components that bridge frozen train and dev are quarantined. A complete initial 768-example synthetic batch was rejected for repetitive templates and artificial item suffixes; its size is not counted as useful training evidence.

Historical trained ledgers are retained unchanged. Separately published stronger prompt-isolation ledgers whitelist 137 independently inspected generic instruction templates, guard remaining instruction/context content and long vocabulary entries, and check short vocabulary only against complete heldout content. Both actual datasets pass the stronger validation. Review declarations in dataset strings alone do not prove model independence; the actual run receipts establish the Muse/Terra division used here.

## Why these experiments

[Staruch et al., BEA 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.bea-1.9/) study adapting language models to minimal-edit correction and error-rate calibration. That supports testing preservation frequency and a conservative final calibration stage, rather than assuming more fluent rewriting means better dictation. Their published model results and hyperparameters are not evidence of an expected gain on Velora.

[GECToR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12592) is another useful design reference: correction can be expressed as small edits rather than unrestricted rewriting. This round keeps LFM3 and its current final-answer decoder; it does not claim to have implemented or benchmarked an edit-tagging model.

[Multi-VALUE](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.44/) provides a framework spanning 50 English dialects and 189 features, with some native-speaker gold evaluations. It motivates testing regional preservation separately from generic American-English correction. Our small authored text challenge does not reproduce its native-speaker evaluation and cannot demonstrate dialect-wide invariance.

[Self-Instruct](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10560) reinforces the need to filter invalid and similar synthetic examples. In this run, rejecting the repetitive first batch and independent full-text reviews were substantive quality controls. More rows remain a hypothesis to test, not a quality guarantee.

Liquid's [official model card](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM3-1.7B/blob/main/README.md) describes 1.73B parameters and a mix of gated short convolutions, sliding-window attention, and global attention. Actual runtime memory and speed are measured on this Mac; model-card sizing and file size do not establish resident memory or a universal speed advantage.

## Locked evaluation and selection

The fresh challenge was frozen before this round's training and baseline output inspection. It has 256 nonterminal model-level cases, plus 52 separately frozen terminal cases. Four ambiguous cases were excluded from scored active evaluation while remaining in the training exclusion guard. An audit of the 52 terminal annotations found 47 exact deterministic bypasses and five Smart Terminal requests that actually use cleanup. Their original blanket bypass label is incorrect; the frozen files are preserved with this erratum and all 52 remain outside the primary nonterminal score. No 52/52 bypass success is claimed. The nonterminal challenge forces the student-model contract; it is not a claim that the application would invoke its model for every short input. The 256 active cases include 32 annotated explicit self-repairs, 61 exact no-edit expectations, and 126 cases with protected literals. A separate 192-case development probe contains 185 supported references, including 43 required repairs. The research author reviewed development gold only and does not judge experiment outputs.

Checkpoints are compared on the same 1,427-example development set. The optional calibration stage must not lose more than two percentage points of required-repair success on the fixed supported development subset. With 43 such cases, one case is 2.33 percentage points, so no net successful-case loss is allowed. Paired regressions are still reported when offset by fixes. Fresh challenge and owner recordings never choose checkpoint, gradients, calibration activations, or a revised recipe. The optional F calibration stage has a separately frozen development objective: complete supported outputs, no net required-repair loss versus both initial C and its selected parent, no increased protected-literal violations, and strictly improved exact no-edit preservation. Eligible F checkpoints maximize that preservation count, with full-dev loss as the tie-breaker; all losses are still reported. This is not a claim that calibration necessarily minimizes likelihood loss.

Owner evaluation keeps all 614 original cases visible in coverage accounting: 605 successful English transcripts, five other-language, three empty/uncertain, and one failed STT. Only 300 English references are supported; the remaining 305 English references are uncertain. Terminal and short-utterance bypasses must be reported separately from model decoding.

The acoustic check uses 24 original VCTK 0.92 recordings: 12 speakers and two speakers each for six publisher labels (Scottish, Northern Irish, Indian, American, South African, Australian). Total audio is 79.27 seconds. It is studio-read speech, four utterances per group, with publisher transcripts rather than new manual audio adjudication. Results are exploratory, not a claim about population accent accuracy. No evaluation audio or owner text enters training or Muse generation.

## Training and checkpoint results

All training uses the audited local LiquidAI/LFM3-1.7B-MLX-8bit snapshot 4d6d94c9c2c2f249f1f740f4f08e071b0dbfe099, the pinned local architecture port, rank-16 LoRA with scale 8, gradient accumulation 4, clipping 1, maximum sequence length 2,048, seed 42, and cosine learning-rate decay. Warm-start experiments use the exact same previous adapter C. Equal 8,192-example budgets do not imply equal token counts, compute, or training time.

Experiment D, the smaller English corpus, took 1,598.5 seconds. Its best checkpoint was after 2,048 examples: full larger-dev loss 0.077733 versus C's 0.103533. Later D checkpoints worsened to 0.088444, 0.095890, and 0.099237, so the final checkpoint is not selected merely because it trained longer.

The first E attempt was interrupted after 410.5 seconds because an overbroad audit falsely classified already-quarantined connectors as an actual split leak. The corrected graph audit found no eligible new unsafe component, and complete re-curation reproduced D/E's rendered model inputs. E restarted from the same original C with unchanged frozen data. The interrupted partial checkpoint is retained, excluded from selection, and its compute cost is accounted for.

E completed in 1,893.3 seconds. Its full-1,427-dev callback losses at the four saved points were 0.056244, 0.054034, 0.052902, 0.053162. Shared checkpoint rescoring and fidelity checks follow.

Shared rescoring selected the cold recipe G checkpoint after 6,144 examples, adapter SHA `a5b141b7e5b261f04f5888d7943bd2a0c2c6d1e3de7e32a0df10040a390c2dd4`, at loss 0.047588. E selected 6,144 at 0.052899 and D selected 2,048 at 0.077733. G starts without C but also uses LR 1e-5 rather than 3e-6, so this does not isolate initialization as the cause. All three recipes have their minimum at approximately one pass over their own unique training corpus. Calibration F and final fidelity checks follow.

The preservation calibration F took 729.4 seconds. Both its 1,000- and 2,000-example checkpoints improved exact no-edit preservation from 83/86 to 84/86, but required-repair success fell from 33/43 to 32/43. Both therefore fail the frozen gate, and G 6,144 is retained for final evaluation. Lower validation loss alone does not establish output quality.

## Inference experiments

The existing C++/Metal prototype, not a new from-scratch inference implementation, is used to isolate data/weight changes from runtime changes. Original MLX and BF16-fused output qualification checks exact input tokens and output boundaries before native conversion. The BF16 export is derived from the 8-bit source and is not a recovery of original unquantized weights.

A unique-single-token copy bootstrap was also rejected before implementation: only 2 of 44 existing public cases had a usable exact anchor, below the predeclared 12-case threshold. The earlier 44-forward ceiling was a theoretical bound, not actual eligible coverage or measured speedup.

The proposed always-copy shortcut was rejected on development evidence before implementation. A restrictive formatting predicate still selected explicit self-corrections that needed repair; making a transcript look finished is insufficient evidence that cleanup can be skipped. Its estimated coverage is not an achieved speed gain.

The 24-clip ASR sanity check has only 159 reference words. Whisper and both Parakeet versions have zero normalized word errors in both reversed rounds, so this set does not rank accuracy. Median transcription-only totals were about 3.364 seconds for Whisper, 1.298 seconds for Parakeet v2, and 1.427 seconds for v3 over the same 79.27 seconds of audio. Preprocessing, load, warmup and memory sampling are separate; these are not end-to-end app times. Parakeet used about 1.28–1.30 GB active MLX memory versus Whisper 1.62 GB, while cold peaks and physical footprint varied across runs. Full owner-audio validation follows.

A metadata-only supplemental search found no >=20-word non-common transcripts among 3,670 eligible VCTK entries for the same 12 speakers (maximum 14). No supplemental audio was downloaded or tested. Total acquisition and recheck payload was 81,786,427 bytes, below 128 MiB. The longer-audio checks therefore use the frozen owner corpus and existing podcast; no longer public-accent corpus is claimed.

All three additional ASR runs processed the full 614-record manifest. On the fixed 300 supported English references (12,105 reference words), frozen Whisper-auto and new Whisper-English each had 422 normalized word errors (3.49%). Parakeet v2 had 1,179 (9.74%), and v3 1,150 (9.50%). Each Parakeet run was worse than Whisper on 193 of 300 paired cases. Insertions were 818/742 versus Whisper 53. These references are model-assisted and number formatting is not normalized; this is a diagnostic, not human-certified semantic accuracy. Parakeet also lacks the existing Whisper glossary behavior. Over the 613 cases successful in every run, summed transcription-call time was 221.389 seconds for frozen Whisper-auto, 202.282 for Whisper-English, 115.249 for v2 and 131.446 for v3. ## Reproducibility and limits

One GPU/model job runs at a time under an exclusive lock. The run has a six-hour ceiling, four distinct training recipes maximum, 8,192 new target-pair candidates maximum, and no new paid API, cloud GPU, dependencies, or model downloads. Development selection, untouched evaluation, and training activation sources remain separate. Model-assisted review is fallible and is not human or acoustic ground truth. Full local receipts and private case outputs remain on this machine; publishable datasets, challenge, source identities, counts, and results accompany this report.

The six-hour wall-clock ceiling was missed. All four training recipes finished before the deadline, but native export, replay and verification continued beyond it. No additional training recipe was started. The user then explicitly requested continuation at 09:39 UTC; remaining fixed comparisons, prompt tests and the already prepared runtime hypothesis resumed in a separately recorded window. The original overrun remains recorded rather than represented as budget compliance. The 410.5-second interrupted E attempt and failed native-header qualification remain in accounting. Existing CLI subscriptions were used; their unmetered cost is unknown. There was no new paid API, cloud GPU, dependency or model download.

The timing audit found large wrapper-versus-inner-call gaps: Whisper-English occupied 4,173.3 seconds in its wrapper while reported case totals summed to 219.3 seconds; warm E fresh occupied 1,345.3 seconds while captured stream totals were 203.0 seconds plus 2.5 seconds load. Full input/tree integrity checks, serialization and lifecycle work fall outside the inner timers; per-case STT flush/fsync is also excluded. The logs do not timestamp every phase, so the dominant residual cost is unknown. Neither job duration nor a stream-only timer is presented as full application latency. Paired96 uses driver monotonic wall around cleanup, including bypasses and finite failures, while excluding STT, load, capture and paste. It is the first 96 frozen cases, not a random sample.

Final `make test` passed on SDK 26.5: **3,935 Swift checks; 2,151 pytest passed, 3 skipped; site, signing, publish-release, bootstrap and 14 update-lifecycle checks passed**. The isolated training suite passed 123 tests earlier in this round. A first final-gate invocation set fake STT globally and correctly triggered two benchmark guard tests; the documented per-test-fixture invocation passed afterward. No fake STT was used for the real audio benchmarks.

All model comparisons above used their recorded source identities; the original baseline/tooling commit is `09e870d`. The source prompt fix and public report are landed afterward. No production model default, app version or release was changed. Owner text/audio and per-case judgments remain local; only public examples, code and aggregate measurements are published.
